The colours of the practice.

Sunlit Study direction Ellie Wheeler, PsyD 19 colours · 2 typefaces

Every colour on the website comes from this list — there are no one-off values anywhere in the build. The palette is deliberately narrow and warm: a paper ground, soft earth for reading, and a single gold reserved for the moments that ask you to act. Click any swatch to copy its hex.

Grounds

The backgrounds. Four warm neutrals, lightest to deepest, used to separate one section from the next without hard edges.

Rules & hairlines

The thin dividing lines between sections and cards. Never used for text or fills.

Type

The reading colours. Full-strength ink for headings, progressively softer earth tones as text becomes secondary.

Accents

Used sparingly and with intent. Gold marks action; sage marks the small labels above each section.

Light and dark

Two sections of the page sit on dark grounds. Accents need lighter versions of themselves there.

Why there are two greens and two golds

An accent that reads on cream disappears on brown.

The services band sits on a soft brown rather than cream. The everyday gold and sage are too dark to read against it, so each has a pale counterpart — Sage Pale and Gold Pale — used on any dark background. The colours stay the same family; only the weight changes, so the section still reads as green labels and gold numerals.

Typefaces

Two families carry the whole site. Both are self-hosted, so nothing is requested from an outside service as the page loads.

Still to resolve

The sage section labels need to darken.

Sage is the small uppercase label above each section — Services, What I help with, and so on. On the pale backgrounds it sits just under the legibility standard, and where it falls on the gold consultation band it is well under. The reassurance line beneath the consultation form is marginal on that same gold.

Everything else clears comfortably, including all body copy, both dark bands and every button. Deepening the sage is a contained change — it touches those labels and nothing else — and is worth doing before launch, since accessibility standards apply to health practice websites.
